# 1. Weak Prompt Analysis

## Weak Prompt

> Write an email about the delayed flight.

## Problems With the Prompt

| Prompt Component | Status | Required Improvement |
|---|---|---|
| Role | Missing | Specify an airline customer-service communications role. |
| Task | Present but vague | Clearly state that the AI must draft a passenger email about the delay. |
| Context | Missing | Provide verified flight, route, time and delay information. |
| Constraints | Missing | Tell the AI not to invent gate, compensation, meal, hotel or policy information. |
| Output Format | Missing | Specify a professional passenger email with a subject line. |
| Tone | Missing | Specify professional, empathetic, calm and reassuring language. |

# 2. Six-Part Structured Prompt

## Role

Act as an airline customer-service communications officer responsible for passenger communication.

## Task

Draft an email informing passengers that Flight AV408 from Delhi to Bengaluru has been delayed by two hours.

## Context

Use only the following verified information:

- Flight: AV408
- Route: Delhi to Bengaluru
- Original departure: 3:00 PM
- Revised departure: 5:00 PM
- Reason: Adverse weather
- Passengers should monitor the airline's official application and airport display screens.

## Constraints

Do not invent or assume:

- Gate number
- Compensation
- Meal vouchers
- Hotel accommodation
- Refund information
- Boarding time
- Other unverified information

Keep the email concise.

## Output Format

Present the response as a professional passenger email with:

- Clear subject line
- Greeting
- Delay information
- Passenger instruction
- Closing

## Tone

Use a professional, empathetic, calm and reassuring tone without making unsupported promises.

---

# 3. Structured Aviation Email

## Subject

Flight AV408 Delay – Delhi to Bengaluru

## Email

Dear Passenger,

Flight AV408 from Delhi to Bengaluru has been delayed by two hours due to adverse weather. The revised departure time is 5:00 PM, instead of the original 3:00 PM.

We understand that changes to your travel plans can be inconvenient and appreciate your patience and understanding.

Please monitor the airline’s official application and the airport display screens for the latest information and any further updates regarding your flight.

We apologize for the inconvenience and thank you for your cooperation.

Regards,  
Airline Customer Service Team

---

# 4. BLUF Structured Prompt

Act as an airline customer-service communication executive.

Draft a professional email informing passengers of Flight AV408 from Delhi to Bengaluru about a two-hour delay caused by adverse weather.

The original departure time was 3:00 PM and the revised departure time is 5:00 PM.

Ask passengers to monitor the airline’s official application and airport display screens for further updates.

Use the BLUF structure.

Keep the email below 130 words.

Use a calm, professional and reassuring tone.

Do not invent a gate number or mention compensation, refunds, meal arrangements, hotel accommodation or other policy benefits.

# 7. Four-Dimension Review Gate

## Accuracy

Verify:

- Flight number
- Route
- Delay duration
- Original departure time
- Revised departure time
- Reason for delay

## Tone

The communication should be:

- Calm
- Professional
- Empathetic
- Reassuring
- Non-exaggerated

## Compliance

Do not include unsupported:

- Compensation
- Refunds
- Meal arrangements
- Hotel accommodation
- Gate information
- Other policy benefits

## Context

The email should:

- Put the important information first
- Give clear passenger instructions
- Direct passengers to official information channels
- Remain concise

# 8. Targeted Follow-Up Prompts

### BLUF

### Verification

### Compliance

### Length

- Flight number

# 10. Passenger-Complaint Response

## Six-Part Prompt

### Role

Act as an airline customer-service communication executive handling passenger complaints.

### Task

Draft a professional response to a passenger who reports:

- A three-hour flight delay
- A missed important meeting
- Unclear information from airport staff

### Context

The passenger states that the flight was delayed for three hours and that airport staff did not provide clear information.

No compensation or refund policy has been provided.

### Constraints

- Acknowledge the inconvenience.
- Show empathy.
- Do not admit legal liability.
- Do not promise compensation.
- Do not promise refunds.
- Do not invent policy information.
- Do not speculate about staff conduct.
- Do not assign blame.
- Direct the passenger to an authorised official customer-service channel.
- Keep the response below 150 words.

### Output Format

Use a professional passenger-service email with:

- Subject
- Greeting
- Acknowledgement
- Support guidance
- Closing

### Tone

Use an empathetic, calm, respectful, professional and non-defensive tone.

---

# 11. Passenger-Complaint Response

## Subject

Regarding Your Flight Delay and Information Concern

Dear Passenger,

Thank you for contacting us. We are sorry to hear about the inconvenience caused by the three-hour flight delay and that the information provided at the airport did not meet your expectations. We understand that the delay affected your plans and caused you to miss an important meeting.

We would like to ensure that your concern is reviewed through the appropriate customer-service process. Please contact the airline through its official customer-service channel and provide your flight and travel details so the matter can be reviewed.

We appreciate you bringing this experience to our attention and apologise for the inconvenience caused.

Regards,  
Airline Customer Service Team

- Passenger inconvenience

It should remain respectful and non-defensive.

## Compliance

Do not:

- Invent policy information

## Context

Direct the passenger to the airline's actual authorised customer-service channel.

# 13. Internal Staff Email

## Six-Part Prompt

### Role

Act as an airport terminal manager communicating with airport staff.

### Task

Draft an internal email informing staff that passenger queues at security screening are longer than usual and that additional staff have been assigned to assist with queue management.

### Context

Security-screening passenger queues are longer than usual.

Additional staff have been assigned to support queue management.

### Constraints

- Use BLUF.
- Do not blame or criticise any department.
- Do not invent causes.
- Do not invent queue times.
- Do not invent staffing numbers.
- Do not invent operational details.
- Give clear instructions.
- Keep the email below 120 words.

### Output Format

Use a concise internal email with:

- Clear subject
- Opening issue statement
- Staff actions
- Closing

### Tone

Use a professional, calm, collaborative and operational tone.

# 19. Reflection

## 1. What are the six parts of a structured email prompt?

The six parts are:

1. Role
2. Task
3. Context
4. Constraints
5. Output Format
6. Tone

They help the AI understand who it should act as, what it should produce, what information it can use, what it must avoid, how the response should be structured, and how it should sound.

## 2. What does BLUF mean?

BLUF means **Bottom Line Up Front**.

It means putting the most important information at the beginning of the communication.

## 3. When is BLUF useful in aviation communication?

BLUF is useful when passengers or staff need important information quickly, such as:

- Flight delays
- Cancellations
- Gate changes
- Operational disruptions
- Urgent internal updates

## 4. What does PAS mean?

PAS stands for:

- Problem
- Agitation
- Solution

It is commonly used in persuasive and marketing communication.

## 5. Why should PAS be used carefully?

PAS should be used carefully in aviation because exaggerating a passenger problem can create unnecessary fear or frustration.

Aviation communication should remain:

- Factual
- Responsible
- Proportionate

## 6. What are the four dimensions of the review gate?

The four dimensions are:

1. Accuracy
2. Tone
3. Compliance
4. Context

They help determine whether AI-generated communication is factually reliable, appropriate in style, compliant with known information and suitable for its audience.

## 7. What unsupported commitments must be avoided?

Avoid unsupported promises about:

- Compensation
- Refunds
- Meal arrangements
- Hotel accommodation
- Gate assignments
- Reimbursements
- Other benefits and policies

## 8. How does the intended audience affect tone?

Passengers generally need:

- Clarity
- Reassurance

Senior management needs:

- Concise factual updates
- Management information

Operations staff need:

- Direct instructions
- Coordination information

## 9. How did peer feedback improve the email?

The peer-review process confirmed that the email was concise and clear and reinforced the need to keep operational information verified.

It also highlighted the importance of:

- Short instructions
- Action-oriented wording
- Avoiding unsupported policy information

## 10. Why must AI-generated business communication receive human approval?

AI can generate fluent text but may:

- Misunderstand context
- Omit important information
- Introduce unsupported details

Human review is therefore necessary to verify:

- Facts
- Operational information
- Policy statements
- Tone
- Privacy
- Organisational requirements

before communication is released.

Effective aviation business communication requires more than grammatically correct AI-generated text.

A professional aviation communication should use:

- Verified information

- Audience-specific tone
- Clear instructions
